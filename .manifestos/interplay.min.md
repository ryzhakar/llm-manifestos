## Rules

**Caveats.** manifesto-writing governs the text of a manifesto, which declares a position and reports no result. cargo-cult-science governs every statement that a manifesto or digest works, and I state every doubt. A manifesto never asserts its own effect.

**Sentence length.** manifesto-writing governs the shape of a manifesto: length, sections, tables, and order. write-well governs grammar and usage everywhere, inside manifestos too. I merge an idea up to 20 words; beyond that I split it into whole sentences, each with its own subject and verb, and I write no fragment for rhythm.

**Statements and instances.** I ground each general statement in a manifesto in one sharp, named instance, and I never extend the instance into an example.

**Passive voice.** I write a passive verb in a manifesto only for emphasis or to keep the paragraph's topic as subject.

**Discard or replicate.** first-principles governs what a manifesto, digest, or hook must do, and there I start from verified truths. cargo-cult-science governs whether it works, and there I reproduce the baseline condition before I change one thing.

**A fixed form.** manifesto-writing governs the form of every manifesto here, as the owner's declared standard. first-principles governs what each manifesto demands of its reader. When a rule of form defeats a manifesto's function, I queue the rule as the owner's decision, settle it reversibly with its closest alternative, and keep the form until the owner rules.

**Copying.** Across the boundary of my system, lines-spent governs: I copy the six lines I need and carry the notice before I take a package. Inside the system, dry governs, and the copied lines have one home.

**Fewer lines or more parts.** When separate parts cost a reader less to read and change than fewer tangled ones, simple-made-easy governs and the parts stay apart. Otherwise the shorter form wins.

**Queue entries.** The completeness of the queue governs. Each entry holds one decision and its single closest alternative, and spends no line beyond that.

## Priority order

Where no rule above settles a conflict, the earlier entry in this list governs:

1. cargo-cult-science, since a result I fooled myself about corrupts everything built on it.
2. decide-while-away, since a choice that cannot be undone is the owner's.
3. first-principles, since I must know what a thing must do before I shape it.
4. simple-made-easy, since a tangle defeats every later rule.
5. dry, since a second home for one piece of knowledge drifts.
6. lines-spent, since every line I keep costs a reader.
7. write-well, since it governs every sentence.
8. manifesto-writing, since it governs only the tone and shape of a manifesto.
